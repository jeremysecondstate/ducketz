# Expanded models and the 70/15/15 split — 2026-09-26

The completed research supports testing the approved chronological 70/15/15 split with the existing four-model ensemble. In the separate control that changes only the split, mean Brier score on identical final timestamps improved from **0.2541468429 to 0.2494674742**. BTC, ETH and HYPE improved; ZEC worsened. The expanded model comparison showed a much smaller gain for a predeclared CNN–GRU blend, and adding every available classical family made the average score worse. None of these results establishes profit after trading costs.

The approved operational scope is the 70/15/15 split for the current logistic, ExtraTrees, HistGradientBoosting and MLP ensemble. New model families remain research candidates. This document records research evidence; it does not certify completion of deployment, a fresh Paper mirror, or subsequent account changes. Those operational steps require their own verification record.

## What 70/15/15 means here

Data are 15-minute completed candles with 38 causal features. The binary target is whether the close exactly four candles later is at least the decision candle's close: a one-hour **not-down** outcome. Unresolved labels and featureless rows are excluded first. The oldest 70% of mature usable rows are allocated to fitting, the next 15% to probability calibration, and the latest 15% to assessment. Fit and calibration counts are floored; rounding remainder goes to assessment. There is no training-row cap or random shuffling of the time split.

Labels whose end time reaches the next block's first decision are purged. This removed four rows at each boundary in these snapshots, so effective counts are slightly below the nominal proportions. For the primary model-family comparison, all candidates also share the same context-valid decision rows. Thirty additional early fitting rows per market lack the full 32-candle input context and are excluded from every primary candidate, including tabular models. No calibration or assessment rows were lost to that filter in this run.

The primary pipeline fits a median imputer and scaler on fitting decisions only. Trees use unscaled imputed features; logistic, MLP and sequence models use standardized features. Arrays are shared as float32. Sequence windows contain the latest 32 observed candles through the decision candle, with no future rows; windows crossing missing-candle gaps are rejected. Calibration uses the later calibration labels, and model recipes, epochs and blend weights are fixed before assessment. The separate production control uses the original per-estimator production pipelines and precision instead of the shared sequence preprocessing.

The full primary assessment covers September 18–26, with 762 BTC observations and 760 observations for each other market. The exact paired comparison uses **288 identical timestamps per market**, from **September 23 at 17:00 UTC through September 26 at 16:45 UTC**. Base fitting ends roughly **381.75–382.5 hours** before the snapshot's latest completed candle because the later 30% is reserved for calibration and assessment. That is an explicit consequence of the chosen split; calibration learns from the middle block.

In the operational pipeline, the latest 15% is a **promotion holdout**: it is excluded from base-model fitting and calibration, but its evaluation can determine qualification. It must not be described as untouched by every model-selection decision. The evaluated bundle has no hidden final refit using assessment labels.

The [classical source audit](C:/dev/ducketz/docs/hyperliquid-system-analysis/audits/2026-09-26-legacy-classical-model-audit.md) covered eight legacy classical families. Reusable estimator families were implemented through a new bounded research factory, without importing or executing legacy scripts. Nine tabular families were available here: the four current members plus RandomForest, AdaBoost, GradientBoosting, LightGBM and XGBoost. CatBoost was explicitly skipped because its optional dependency is absent; it was not installed for this experiment.

The [neural source audit](C:/dev/ducketz/docs/hyperliquid-system-analysis/audits/2026-09-26-legacy-neural-model-audit.md) found **13 variants in 12 folders**, including two transformer variants. Three compact representatives were implemented for this comparison rather than porting all legacy networks: CNN, GRU and CNN–GRU. With 38 features, their recorded parameter counts are **2,641**, **2,705** and **3,489**, respectively. These counts match all four market reports. Each uses eight fixed epochs, batches of 128, Adam at 0.001, a 32-candle past window and CPU execution.

## Interpretation limits and operational handoff

This is one retrospective period per market. The four markets are related and one-hour labels overlap across adjacent 15-minute decisions. Earlier research already inspected parts of this history, so neither the primary assessment nor the supplemental comparison is a globally untouched test. The supplement addresses a disclosed comparison confound; it does not create new independent market evidence. No statistical-significance claim follows from the displayed mean differences.

Neither experiment executes a trading policy or evaluates fees, funding, slippage, turnover or resulting account equity. Forecast accuracy and calibration can improve while a policy still loses after costs. A fresh, verified Paper start and subsequent forward observations are required to judge the approved operational adjustment. Deployment of the 70/15/15 split is limited to the existing four-model ensemble; the additional classical and sequence families remain isolated research code and results.

The research scripts write beneath the datastore's `_model_research` directory and report no live-state mutation. This audit's verification read frozen files and recomputed metrics only. The separate [completed deployment and BTC-inclusive mirror receipt](2026-09-26-701515-btc-fresh-mirror.md) records active model revisions, effective split counts, qualification results, the fresh account snapshot and the verified operations handoff.
